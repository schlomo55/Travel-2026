Chongqing is the mountain megacity — 8D streets where a "ground floor" door can open onto a rooftop. Don't fight it with maps too much; the city rewards wandering between levels.

**Hongyadong** at night is the postcard: a cliff of stacked wooden pavilions in lantern light. Go after dark (lights typically until late evening — *verify locally*), see it once from across the river for the full face, then once inside for the food floors. The **Yangtze River cableway** nearby is a short ride with the best skyline angle — the queue moves faster than it looks in December.

Hotpot is the city religion, and Chongqing spice is a different sport: 微辣 ("wei la", mild) is already serious — order the split pot (鸳鸯锅) with a clear side without shame; every table around you will understand. Numbing (麻) from Sichuan pepper is normal and passes. December is mild here (8–12°C), often misty — the haze actually flatters the neon.
