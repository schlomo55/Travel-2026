## ⭐ Our picks
- ⭐ **798 Art District** (Dashanzi) — Bauhaus factory halls turned into galleries and cafés. Indoor, warm, free to wander, and a completely different Beijing from the imperial one. Half a day *(verify which galleries are open, some close between shows)*
- ⭐ **Beihai Park** — the White Dagoba above a frozen lake, ten minutes on foot from Jingshan. The natural second half of a Forbidden City morning
- ⭐ **Gulou at opening time** — climb the Drum or Bell Tower for the hutong rooftops, then eat your way south through Baochao and Doujiao hutongs
- ⭐ **The National Museum on Tiananmen Square** — free, enormous and warm; the grey-day answer that never disappoints *(verify the current booking rule — passport entry, usually reserved in advance)*
- ⭐ **Nanluoguxiang's side lanes, not its main drag** — the parallel hutongs east and west are where the quiet courtyard bars are

## ➕ More options
- **Beijing Ancient Observatory** — Ming and Qing bronze instruments on a surviving city-wall tower, ten minutes from Jianguomen
- **Ming Tombs and the Sacred Way** — the tree-lined spirit road with its stone animals; often combined with a Great Wall day and best in bare winter light
- **Panjiayuan antique market** — the real version happens on weekend mornings; bargaining expected *(verify the weekday hours, most good stalls are Sat–Sun)*
- **Sanlitun Taikoo Li** in the evening — modern Beijing, international food, and the district your first hotel is in
- **Capital Museum** — the city's own story, quieter than the national one and just as warm *(verify booking)*
- **Wangfujing** for the church, the bookshop street and an indoor hour when the wind is unbearable
- Foggy or freezing alternate: the **Palace Museum's own galleries** — ceramics, clocks and the treasure halls are all indoors and included in the ticket
- **Caochangdi or Songzhuang** if 798 leaves you wanting more art *(verify what is open — many spaces shut for winter)*
