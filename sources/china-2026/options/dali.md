## ⭐ Our picks
- ⭐ **Walk the old town gate to gate** — south to north takes twenty minutes and hands you the whole plan of the place before you eat anything
- ⭐ **Xizhou**, the Bai merchant town 20 km north — painted courtyard mansions and open fields. The best half day out of Dali *(verify the bus, or share a taxi)*
- ⭐ **Erhai's west shore at sunset** — Caicun dock is the closest piece of lake to the old town, about fifteen minutes by taxi
- ⭐ **One quiet courtyard teahouse** — Dali's real skill, and there are dozens a lane back from Renmin Road
- ⭐ **Keep the morning of the 20th unhurried but not open-ended** — the train to Kunming is the last moving part of the visa plan

## ➕ More options
- **Zhonghe cableway** instead of Ximatan — closer to the old town, cheaper, lower, and it keeps running when the top is windy *(verify which lines are open that day)*
- **Dali Museum**, in the old Qing yamen — small, warm and free or nearly so *(verify opening days)*
- **The Catholic church on Renmin Road** — a Bai-style timber church with upturned eaves. An odd and lovely five minutes
- **Bai tie-dye at Zhoucheng**, next to Xizhou — indigo dyeing you do yourself *(verify a workshop is running that day)*
- **Shuanglang** on the far shore — prettier than Caicun, but an hour's drive; only if a whole morning is free
- **Foreigner Street** (Huguo Road) — the old backpacker strip, now mostly cafés. Good coffee, no illusions
- **Erhai by bike** — the lakeside path is flat and bikes are everywhere; the December wind decides whether it is fun
- Rainy alternate: the halls of **Chongsheng Temple** behind the Three Pagodas are large and mostly indoors

## 🍜 Where to eat
- ⭐ **Renmin Road food stalls** on arrival — **饵块 erkuai**, grilled cheese, 凉鸡米线. You come off a 4–5 hour car ride from the gorge; this is the right, low-effort first meal
- ⭐ **梅子井酒家 Meizi Well**, Renmin Road — clay-pot fish, in a traditional Bai courtyard
- **我在大理等你** ("I'm in Dali waiting for you"), Renmin Road — wild-mushroom hotpot and Dali dishes
- **再回首**, Renmin Road — the spicy cold noodles with chicken strips that locals actually name
- **Bai specialities worth ordering**: 砂锅鱼 clay-pot fish, 酸辣鱼 sour-and-spicy fish, 梅子扣肉 plum-scented pork, tile-grilled fish, and **凉虾 "cold shrimp"** — a sweet iced rice-paste dessert shaped like tiny shrimp
- **Bai three-course tea at Zhoucheng** — the Dec 20 morning option, and it is indoors and warm
